The studio

A French Compagnon du Devoir, trained on the Tour de France, building in Brooklyn.

Robin Mielleux learned iron and wood on the road. At seventeen he left for the Tour de France of the Compagnons du Devoir, the French guild whose apprentices change city and workshop every year: forge in Lyon, art metalwork in Strasbourg, cabinetmaking in Bordeaux, steel framing in Nantes, then Tours, Angers and Marseille. He was admitted as a Compagnon in 2016 after his reception piece, a steel and walnut console built without a single screw or drop of glue.

In 2019 he moved to New York and set up in a former shipping warehouse in Red Hook. The work changed scale with the city: railings for four-storey brownstone stairs, twelve-foot dining tables for Tribeca lofts, floor-to-ceiling libraries on the Upper West Side. The method did not. Steel carries, wood receives, brass marks the joints. Nothing is hidden: a through-tenon stays visible, a rivet stays a rivet.

“A true joint doesn’t need decorating. It needs to be true.”

Robin Mielleux, Compagnon metalworker & cabinetmaker
